Chapter 01

The arrival

Hotels in Mérida received 2.26 million tourists in 2025, 30% more than before the pandemic. One in five comes from abroad, a share that has barely moved: the growth is national and foreign alike.

Chapter 02

The Centro empties out

Between 2019 and 2025 the Centro Histórico lost 1,189 businesses while the rest of the city gained them. Coffee shops are the exception: up 46% citywide, spreading to 212 neighbourhoods outside the old core.

Chapter 03

The invisible hotel

Registered lodging barely changed: 304 places in 2019, 311 in 2025. Yet the state collected 3.2 times more lodging tax. The gap points to homes rented by the night, the part of the hotel industry the business directory cannot see.

Chapter 05

Not Cancún, not yet

Mérida's airport is still mostly domestic and grows faster than Cancún's. But homes in Yucatán already cost more than in Quintana Roo or Oaxaca. The tourism is smaller; the pressure on housing is not.
